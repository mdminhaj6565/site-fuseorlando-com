Thermostat Repair in Orlando

When the thermostat stops working, the whole heating and cooling system stops listening. In an Orlando summer that can turn a comfortable home into an uncomfortable one within an hour. The good news is that many thermostat problems are quick to diagnose and fix. The important part is confirming whether the thermostat is really at fault or whether it is reporting a problem somewhere else in the system. Fuse HVAC & Appliance Repair troubleshoots and repairs thermostats and control wiring for homes and businesses across Orlando and Central Florida.

Common Thermostat Problems

  • Blank or dim display. Dead batteries, a tripped breaker, a blown fuse on the air handler control board, or a missing C-wire on a smart thermostat.
  • AC or heat will not turn on. Wrong mode, loose wires, a failed relay in the thermostat or a safety switch (such as a float switch on a clogged drain) cutting power.
  • System will not shut off. A stuck relay, shorted wire or incorrect configuration.
  • Temperature reading is wrong. Thermostat placed in sunlight, near a supply vent or on an exterior wall, or a failing sensor.
  • Short cycling. The system turns on and off every few minutes due to a bad location, wrong cycle settings or an oversized system.
  • Heat pump heats in cooling mode. Incorrect O/B reversing valve setting after a replacement.
  • Smart thermostat keeps rebooting or losing Wi-Fi. Power problems, usually related to the C-wire.

Quick Checks You Can Do First

  1. Replace the thermostat batteries if it uses them.
  2. Confirm the mode is set to Cool or Heat and the set point is below or above room temperature.
  3. Check the AC and air handler breakers in the panel.
  4. Look at the condensate drain safety switch near the indoor unit; if the pan is full of water, the switch may have shut the system off.
  5. Make sure the air filter is not completely clogged.

If these checks do not solve it, turn the system off and call us. Repeatedly cycling power can make some faults worse.

How We Diagnose Thermostat Problems

Our technicians test the thermostat and the system together. We check the low-voltage transformer and fuse on the control board, measure voltage at the thermostat terminals, and jumper the control circuits to see if the equipment responds without the thermostat. This tells us quickly whether the problem is the thermostat, the wiring or the equipment. If the fault is in the AC itself, such as a failed capacitor or contactor, our AC repair team can fix it on the same visit.

Repair or Replace the Thermostat?

We repair wiring faults, loose connections, configuration mistakes and placement problems. When the thermostat itself has failed, replacement is usually faster and more cost-effective than repair. If your thermostat is more than 10 years old or cannot be scheduled, a new programmable or smart model is often worth it. See thermostat installation for options.

Thermostat Problems That Point to Bigger Issues

Sometimes the thermostat is simply the messenger:

  • The AC runs constantly but the house never cools – low refrigerant, a dirty coil or a failing compressor. See air conditioning repair.
  • Heat strips run all the time in winter – a heat pump problem. See heat pump services.
  • Rooms at very different temperatures – duct leaks or poor airflow. See ductwork installation and air duct cleaning.
  • Breaker trips when the system starts – an electrical fault. See circuit breaker replacement.

Preventing Thermostat Problems

Regular AC maintenance includes checking thermostat operation, low-voltage wiring and the condensate safety switch that often causes "dead thermostat" calls in Florida. Members of our residential maintenance plan get two seasonal checks a year.

Thermostat Wiring Problems We Find Most Often

Thermostat wiring runs through walls and attics where it can be damaged by rodents, pinched by insulation work or loosened over time. We regularly find broken conductors, corroded connections at the air handler, and wires that were reused incorrectly after a DIY replacement. On heat pump systems, one wrong wire can prevent the system from switching between heating and cooling. Our technicians trace the circuit end to end, repair or replace damaged wire and document the correct wiring for future reference.

Heat Pump and Emergency Heat Questions

If your thermostat shows "aux heat" or "emergency heat" often, the heat pump may not be keeping up on its own. A short period of auxiliary heat on the coldest mornings is normal, but constant aux heat usually means a problem with the heat pump, the defrost cycle or thermostat settings, and it can raise electric bills quickly. We check the thermostat configuration and the heat pump together so the cause is fixed, not just the symptom.

Thermostat Repair FAQ

Why does my thermostat say "cool on" but no air comes out?

The thermostat is calling for cooling, but the indoor blower or outdoor unit is not responding. Common causes are a tripped float switch, a blown control fuse or a failed capacitor.

Can a bad thermostat increase my electric bill?

Yes. Incorrect readings, short cycling or aux heat running unnecessarily all waste energy.
