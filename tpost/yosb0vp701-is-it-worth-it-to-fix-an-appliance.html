When to Consider Appliance Repair?

Before deciding to replace a broken appliance, it's important to weigh the benefits of professional appliance repair. In many cases, repairing your appliance can save you a significant amount of money compared to buying a new one. Here are a few factors to consider:

Age of the Appliance

If your appliance is less than 8-10 years old, chances are good that a repair will extend its life for several more years. Most appliances, including refrigerators, dishwashers, washers, and dryers, are designed to last at least a decade. Appliance Repair Cost vs. Replacement Cost

One of the main concerns homeowners have is appliance repair cost. A general rule of thumb: if the cost of repairing the appliance is less than 50% of the price of a new one, it's usually worth fixing. Warranty Considerations

If your appliance is still under warranty, repair is almost always the best option. Most warranties cover parts and labor, making appliance repair Orlando Florida even more affordable. Our certified technicians can handle warranty repairs for many top brands.

Common Appliances Worth Repairing

Some appliances are more economical to repair than others. Here are a few where repair often makes the most sense:
  • Refrigerators: Refrigerators are expensive to replace and essential to your home. Often, issues like faulty thermostats or compressors can be fixed quickly by Orlando appliance repair experts.
  • Washing Machines and Dryers: These appliances are used frequently, and simple repairs (like replacing belts or pumps) can keep them running smoothly.
  • Dishwashers: Problems such as clogged drains or malfunctioning control boards are usually fixable.
  • Ovens and Stoves: Repairing a stove or oven is typically cheaper than replacing it, especially if the issue is with heating elements or igniters.

Signs You Should Replace Instead of Repair

While many problems are fixable, there are situations where replacement may be more practical:
  • High Appliance Repair Cost: If the repair cost exceeds 50% of the appliance's value.
  • Frequent Breakdowns: If your appliance breaks down often, ongoing repair bills may outweigh the cost of a new unit.
  • Obsolete Parts: If the necessary parts are no longer available or are extremely costly.
  • Energy Inefficiency: Older appliances may use more energy, so upgrading to a modern, energy-efficient model could save money in the long run.
