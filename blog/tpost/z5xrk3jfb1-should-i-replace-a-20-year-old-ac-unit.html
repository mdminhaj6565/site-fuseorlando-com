Most AC units have a typical lifespan of 10–15 years with proper maintenance. Once your air conditioner hits the 15 to 20-year mark, you’re likely dealing with outdated technology, worn-out components, and increasing inefficiency. In the HVAC Orlando, FL climate—where systems work overtime during long summers—wear and tear accumulate even faster.

Top Signs It's Time to Replace Your Old AC Unit

Not sure when to replace your old AC unit? Here are typical red flags:
1. Frequent Breakdowns: If your system requires frequent repairs, the maintenance cost mounts up fast. HVAC replacement in Orlando soon becomes more economical than repairing an old system.
2. Higher Energy Bills: Old systems use outdated technology and operate at lower SEER (Seasonal Energy Efficiency Ratio) ratings. New systems are much more efficient, and you'll save money on utility bills every month.
3. Uneven Cooling: Is your home always too hot or too cold? A failing AC unit can't properly regulate temperature and humidity—especially in Orlando's humid climate.
4. Strange Noises or Odors: Grinding, squealing, musty smells, or burning odors can be signs of motor failure, mold growth, or worn components—indicators that Orlando HVAC replacement time has arrived.
5. Outdated Refrigerant: If your system still runs on R-22 refrigerant (Freon), consider replacement highly recommended. R-22 is being phased out, so it's getting more expensive and difficult to obtain.

Should I Repair or Replace My 20-Year-Old AC?

Here’s how to decide whether to repair or replace your old AC unit:
  • Unit Age:
Repair if under 10 years old
Replace if over 15–20 years old
  • Repair Cost:
Repair if cost is under $500
Replace if repair cost exceeds $1,500
  • Energy Efficiency:
Repair if the unit still has a decent SEER rating
Replace if the system is inefficient and driving up energy bills
  • Frequency of Breakdowns:
Repair if issues are rare
Replace if breakdowns are frequent or recurring
  • Comfort & Performance:
Repair if the system still cools effectively
Replace if comfort levels are poor and rooms are unevenly cooled
