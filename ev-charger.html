EV Charger Installation in Orlando

Charging an electric vehicle from a standard household outlet adds only a few miles of range per hour. A Level 2 charger on a dedicated 240-volt circuit can refill a typical daily commute in a couple of hours and a nearly empty battery overnight. Fuse HVAC & Appliance Repair installs home and commercial EV charging across Orlando and Central Florida, starting with the part that matters most: making sure your electrical system can support the charger safely.

EV charger installation is part of our electrical services, alongside panel upgrades and breaker replacement.

Level 1 vs. Level 2 Charging

Charging levelPower sourceTypical use
Level 1Standard 120V outletOccasional top-ups, plug-in hybrids, very short commutes
Level 2Dedicated 240V circuitDaily charging at home or work for most battery EVs
DC fast chargingCommercial three-phase servicePublic and fleet charging stations

For most Orlando drivers, a Level 2 charger is the right choice. It works with Tesla, Ford, Chevrolet, Hyundai, Kia, Rivian, BMW and every other major brand, either directly or with the adapter supplied by the manufacturer.

Our EV Charger Installation Process

  1. Site review. We look at where you park, where the panel is, the wall or pedestal location and the cable length you need.
  2. Load calculation. We add up your existing loads, including central air, electric cooking, the dryer and water heater, to confirm how much capacity is available for the charger.
  3. Recommendation. You get options: a hardwired charger or a 240V outlet, the right amperage for your vehicle and panel, and whether load management or a panel upgrade is needed.
  4. Installation. We install the dedicated circuit, breaker and charger or outlet, with conduit and weather protection for outdoor locations.
  5. Permit, inspection and testing. Where required, the installation is permitted and inspected. We test the charger with your vehicle when possible and show you how to use its app or scheduling features.

Choosing the Right Charger

  • Amperage. A 40A circuit delivering 32A of charging covers most daily needs. Larger vehicles or longer commutes may benefit from a 48A charger on a 60A circuit if the panel allows it.
  • Hardwired or plug-in. Hardwired chargers allow higher power and are well suited to outdoor installs. A NEMA 14-50 outlet is flexible if you may change chargers later.
  • Smart features. Wi-Fi chargers let you schedule charging for off-peak hours and track energy use.
  • Location. Indoor garage walls are simplest. For driveways and carports we use outdoor-rated equipment suitable for Florida rain and heat.

When Your Panel Needs Help

Many Orlando homes built before the 2000s have 100A or 125A service, and some newer homes have panels that are already full. Options include:

  • A load management device that pauses charging when other large loads run.
  • A sub-panel to add breaker space near the garage.
  • A main panel or service upgrade to 200A.

We explain the cost and benefit of each option so you can choose what fits your budget and future plans, such as a second EV or solar panels.

Commercial and Multi-Unit EV Charging

Businesses, HOAs and property managers increasingly need charging for employees, residents and fleet vehicles. We install commercial Level 2 stations, plan circuits for multiple chargers and coordinate with your existing electrical service. If your building also needs commercial HVAC service, our team can handle both.

EV Charging Safety Tips

  • Avoid using extension cords with a Level 1 or Level 2 charger.
  • Use chargers that are safety-listed by a recognized testing lab.
  • Keep the charging cable off the ground where it can be driven over or sit in standing water.
  • If the charger faults repeatedly or the breaker trips, have the circuit inspected rather than resetting it again and again.

How Long Does Charging Take?

Charging speed depends on the charger's output and the car's onboard charger. As a rough guide, a Level 2 charger delivering 32 amps adds roughly 25 to 30 miles of range per hour for many EVs, while a 48-amp charger adds more if the car can accept it. For most Orlando commuters who drive 30 to 50 miles a day, a few hours of overnight charging is enough to start every morning with a full daily range. We help you choose an amperage that matches your car and driving instead of paying for capacity you will not use.

Preparing Your Home for a Second EV

Many households add a second electric vehicle within a few years. If that is likely, it is worth planning now: a slightly larger circuit, a charger that supports load sharing, or conduit sized for a future run can save a second installation later. Load-sharing chargers split available power between two vehicles so both can charge overnight without a service upgrade.

EV Charger FAQ

How long does EV charger installation take?

Most residential installations with a nearby panel are completed in a few hours. Longer cable runs or panel upgrades take more time and are quoted in advance.

Can you install a Tesla Wall Connector?

Yes. We install Tesla Wall Connectors and universal J1772 or NACS chargers for all EV brands.

Do I need a permit for an EV charger in Orlando?

A new dedicated circuit for an EV charger typically requires a permit. We tell you what applies to your job before work begins.

Will an EV charger raise my electric bill a lot?

It depends on your driving. Scheduling charging for off-peak hours through a smart charger helps reduce costs.
